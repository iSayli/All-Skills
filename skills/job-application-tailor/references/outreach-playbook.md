# Outreach Playbook — Recruiters & Hiring Managers

Default tone: **direct, confident, and personal.** Sayli leads with a specific, real reason she's reaching out and a specific, real reason she's a fit — no generic flattery, no groveling. Her one sanctioned courtesy opener is "I hope you don't mind the direct note." (see template B); don't add any other throat-clearing. Every message references one true, specific thing about the company, role, or person. Short. One clear ask.

## Universal rules
- **One specific hook, every time.** Name the actual product, the actual problem space, or a real detail from the JD/company — never "I love your mission." If there's no specific hook available, ask Sayli for one rather than writing filler.
- **Lead with fit, not need.** Open on what she brings to *their* problem (e.g. AI human-in-the-loop UX for a fintech tool), not on what she wants.
- **One ask.** A 15-minute chat, a referral, or "is this role still open?" — pick one.
- **No metric soup.** Cite at most one verified result (from `profile.md`), chosen to match their problem.
- **Channel-fit length.** LinkedIn connection note ≤ 300 chars. Cold email / LinkedIn message: 5 short paragraphs per template B (~180–250 words). Follow-ups: 2–4 sentences.
- **Never fabricate a connection, referral, or shared contact.** If Sayli has a real connection, use it; otherwise don't imply one.
- **Confident, not arrogant.** "I'd be a strong fit because I've shipped X" — backed by a real artifact — not "I'm the best candidate."

## Sponsorship handling (F-1 visa, STEM OPT, needs sponsorship)
- **Do NOT volunteer sponsorship in a first cold outreach** unless the posting explicitly screens for it or asks — it's not the opener's job to pre-disqualify. Lead with fit.
- **Do state it honestly and early when:** the application form asks, the recruiter asks, the JD says "must be authorized without sponsorship now or in future," or it's a screening call. Frame it factually: for US roles she is on an F-1 visa with STEM OPT and will require sponsorship for continued employment. No apology.
- **Never imply citizenship or green card.** If unsure whether to mention it in a given message, default to omitting it from cold outreach and including it the moment authorization comes up.
- Sample line when asked: *"I'm on an F-1 visa with STEM OPT and will need visa sponsorship to continue beyond it. Happy to share specifics on timing."*

## Message types & templates
Templates are scaffolds — always swap in a real, specific hook. Pull at most one verified metric.

### A — LinkedIn connection note (≤300 chars)
> Hi [Name] — I design AI-powered, decision-support tools (most recently consulting on a multi-agent investigation platform). Saw the [role] on [team] and the [specific product/problem] caught my eye. Would love to connect and learn how your team approaches [specific thing].

### B — Cold email / LinkedIn message (Sayli's proven format — DEFAULT)
This is Sayli's own format, used for Reevo and Honeywell. Use it for every cold email or LinkedIn message unless she asks for something else. Write in her voice: first person, plain, warm, a little informal ("Check out how I work here"), no corporate filler.

**Structure — 5 short paragraphs:**
1. **Opener + identity + hook.** "I hope you don't mind the direct note. I'm Sayli, a Product Designer with an MS in HCI from Georgia Tech and a background in computer engineering." Then name the exact role and ONE hook, either:
   - a specific line from the posting that stood out to her (quote or closely paraphrase it), or
   - what she believes is core to the company's products, tied to the kind of work she cares about (e.g. Honeywell → decision-making tools for people reading complex systems under high stakes).
2. **Proof 1 — the most relevant recent work, ideally Arbiter.** What she designed, in human terms (e.g. how journalists steer, correct, and verify AI agents), plus ONE shipped outcome and/or an adoption signal. Tie it back to the hook.
3. **Proof 2 — "I went past design."** Technical depth from the GT fintech lab: she defined a lightweight LLM document-parsing pipeline for syndicated credit agreements using Cursor, so she could design the interface around its uncertain AI outputs. Keep this paragraph about the pipeline, not about review-time metrics. End with the case-study link: "You can see how I work here: https://www.saylipednekar.com/work/creditseer" (swap to a better-matched case study only if one clearly fits the company better).
4. **Honest gap + bridge.** "I haven't [worked in / designed for] [their domain or users] yet, but I've learned credit analysts' and journalists' workflows from the ground up, and I'd like to do the same with [their specific users]." Name their real users (SDRs, AEs, and RevOps; people running plants, buildings, and process systems; etc.).
5. **One ask.** To an employee/designer: "If you think my background could be a strong fit for the team, I would truly appreciate you passing along my profile." To a recruiter: swap for a request for a quick 15-minute call.

Sign-off: "Best,\nSayli". Subject line: "[Role] — [short phrase echoing the hook]".

**Accuracy guardrails for this format (these phrasings have been checked against `profile.md`):**
- Arbiter shipped metric: search-plan failures dropped from about 46% of attempts to near zero after launch. If it comes up on a call, the evidence is SimPPL's PostHog analysis over 90 days (94 users through the flow).
- Arbiter adoption signal: "My interaction patterns are now cited in the team's Design Standards as the examples to follow." Do NOT say her Claude skills or audit frameworks are "documented as the team's standard" — only the two UI patterns (leave-case-study dialog, inline form errors) are. Her Claude skills can be mentioned separately: "and I built reusable Claude skills for heuristic evaluation and design-system audits."
- Keep the one-metric rule: the Arbiter metric is the one; don't add the 50% review-time number in the same email.

### C — Follow-up after applying (2–4 sentences)
> Hi [Name] — I applied for the [role] on [date] and wanted to flag my interest directly. [One specific reason the fit is unusually good — a domain or AI-UX overlap.] I'd welcome the chance to talk through how I'd approach [their specific problem]. Portfolio: saylipednekar.com.

### D — No-response nudge (after ~1 week, 2–3 sentences)
> Hi [Name] — circling back on the [role]. Still very interested, and since I last wrote I [one new, true, relevant data point — shipped X, published Y]. Happy to make it easy to evaluate fit — quick call or any materials you need?

### E — Thank-you after a call/interview (3–5 sentences)
> Hi [Name] — thanks for the conversation about [specific topic discussed]. It clarified [specific thing], and I left more excited about [specific problem on their team]. [One sentence connecting a real Sayli strength to something they raised.] Looking forward to next steps.

## "Why this company / role" paragraph (for cover letters or forms)
Connect a real Sayli value or interest to a specific, true company detail. Structure: (1) the specific thing about them, (2) the real overlap with her work/values, (3) what she'd bring to their specific problem. No "passionate about your mission" without naming the mission and why it maps to her.
