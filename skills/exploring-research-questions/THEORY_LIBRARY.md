# Theory Library

A curated list of high-leverage theories by domain. Use these to ground research questions.

## Information & attention
- **Agenda-setting theory** (McCombs & Shaw) — media salience shapes public salience
- **Information overload** (Eppler & Mengis) — excess info degrades decision quality
- **Selective exposure** (Zillmann & Bryant) — users prefer attitude-consistent information
- **Two-step flow** (Katz & Lazarsfeld) — opinion leaders mediate mass media effects

## Platforms & networks
- **Network externalities** (Katz & Shapiro) — value scales with adoption
- **Preferential attachment** (Barabási & Albert) — rich-get-richer in network growth
- **Social contagion** (Centola) — behavior spreads through network ties
- **Context collapse** (Marwick & boyd) — multiple audiences flatten context in public posts

## Behavior & decision-making
- **Prospect theory** (Kahneman & Tversky) — losses loom larger than gains
- **Construal level theory** (Trope & Liberman) — psychological distance shapes abstraction
- **Dual process theory** (Kahneman) — System 1 vs. System 2 cognition
- **Reactance theory** (Brehm) — restrictions motivate the restricted behavior
- **Moral disengagement** (Bandura) — mechanisms that allow norm-violating behavior

## Trust & credibility
- **Elaboration likelihood model** (Petty & Cacioppo) — central vs. peripheral cue processing
- **Source credibility model** (Hovland) — expertise + trustworthiness drive persuasion
- **Trust transfer** (Stewart) — trust in one entity transfers to affiliated entities
- **Signaling theory** (Spence) — costly signals credibly reveal private information

## Institutions & norms
- **Institutional theory** (DiMaggio & Powell) — organizations conform to field-level norms
- **Social norms theory** (Cialdini) — descriptive vs. injunctive norms drive behavior
- **Broken windows theory** (Wilson & Kelling) — visible disorder signals permissiveness

## ML / AI-specific
- **Goodhart's Law** — when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure
- **Algorithmic aversion / appreciation** (Dietvorst; Logg) — when humans defer to or reject algorithms
- **Concept drift** — distribution shift over time undermines deployed model validity
- **Shortcut learning** (Geirhos et al.) — models exploit spurious correlations

## Instructions for use
For each candidate RQ, pick the most load-bearing theory — the one whose *mechanism* directly explains the phenomenon you are studying. Do not cite theories superficially. The theory should generate a *specific prediction* that your data can test.
